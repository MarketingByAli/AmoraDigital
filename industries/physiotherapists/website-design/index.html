The real cost

A weak physio website loses new client bookings before the treatment room opens

A runner with a knee twinge searches on the train and compares who explains sports rehab, direct access without a GP letter and insurance in plain language. Practices that bury DTF on a PDF or hide which therapist handles dry needling lose the intake to the site that shows treatment rooms and tomorrow’s slots on one scroll.

Physio practices do not need a dentist-style brochure or a restaurant menu. They need a digital front desk: specialisation pages, body-area complaint paths, direct-access explanation, reimbursement clarity and a booking flow that works while someone is still deciding after a training injury. Miss that, and you pay in empty mid-week slots and overloaded phones on Monday mornings.

Google Business Profile and referrals from GPs can create awareness, but your website is where you control the story: which therapist fits which complaint, how direct access works, and how a Maps click becomes a confirmed intake without five emails to reception.

What we build

What is included in a physiotherapy website

Each block answers what clients ask before the first treatment room slot: DTF rules, insurance, which specialisation fits — not a clinic skin pasted over standard pages.

Online appointment booking

Clients book an intake or follow-up without calling during treatment hours. Choose therapist, complaint type and preferred slot — confirmations go to practice and client automatically.

Treatments & specialisations

Clear pages for manual therapy, sports physio, dry needling, rehabilitation and more — so someone with a sports injury does not land on a generic “services” block that could belong to any clinic.

Direct access (DTF) explained

Explain that clients can start without a GP referral where direct access applies, what screening looks like, and when you still recommend seeing a doctor first — fewer confused calls at reception.

Insurance & reimbursement info

Plain-language pages on supplementary insurance, session coverage and self-pay options. Clients decide whether to book before they ask reception the same three questions every morning.

Complaints by body area

Paths for back, neck, shoulder, knee and other common complaints that lead to the right specialisation and booking CTA — matching how people search when they are already in pain.

Exercises & helpful content

Space for exercise tips, recovery blogs or video explainers that build trust and support SEO — without turning the site into a random health magazine that forgets the book button.

Mobile-first booking path

Most “physio near me” searches happen on a phone after training or work. The site loads fast, shows whether you accept new clients and keeps “Book appointment” sticky.

Client reviews & GP trust

Highlight client reviews next to booking CTAs, and make it easy for referring GPs or coaches to find your specialisations and contact path.

How we work

How a physiotherapy website project runs

From the first walkthrough of your intake and specialisations to measuring which pages drive bookings.

01

Discovery in the practice

We map how new clients find you: GP referral, Google, sports clubs and self-referral via DTF. We note specialisations, caseload and where bookings get stuck between phone and planner.

02

Specialisations & booking structure

We organise by complaint and specialisation: sports shoulder, chronic back, post-surgery rehab — each with its own path to intake booking and DTF explanation. Insurance and reimbursement sit where clients read before abandoning the form.

03

Design for movement & trust

Visual design that feels active and calm: readable on a phone in the changing room, CTAs that say “Book an appointment”, and space for DTF and insurance answers without clutter.

04

Build, integrate, train

We build the site, connect the appointment planner you use, publish reimbursement and DTF pages, and train the team to update specialisations or “accepting clients” status without a developer.

05

Launch & measure bookings

Go live measuring intake form starts, insurance-page exits and which body-area landing pages fill Monday slots. You learn if club referrals or Maps searches drive treatment-room occupancy — not idle browsers who never needed a physio.

Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers on SEO, AI SEO, Local SEO and digital growth from Amora Digital.

How much does a website for a physio practice cost?
Cost depends on how many specialisations you present, whether you need an integrated online planner, DTF and reimbursement pages, multilingual content and practice photography. After a short intake we send a fixed quote by package — Essential, booking-ready or multi-location — not open-ended hourly billing mid-build.
Can clients book an appointment online?
Yes. We connect the appointment planner your practice already uses, or set up a clear booking path for intakes and follow-ups so clients book without calling during treatment hours.
How do I attract new clients through a physio website?
Show specialisations clearly, explain direct access and reimbursement, add client reviews, and make booking one tap away. Local SEO and ads can bring traffic later; the website must convert that traffic into agenda slots.
Can I show my specialisations on the website?
Yes — and you should. We build dedicated paths for manual therapy, sports physio, dry needling, rehabilitation and other specialisations so clients and referring GPs find the right therapist fast.
Should the site explain direct access without a referral (DTF)?
Yes when you offer it. Clear DTF copy reduces confusion at reception and helps clients who search after an injury understand they can often start without a GP letter — while still noting when medical advice comes first.
How long does a physiotherapy website project take?
Most practice websites launch in about four to eight weeks, depending on how ready specialisation content and team photos are, and which booking tool we integrate. Discovery starts with your intake and caseload so we do not build a generic clinic template.
Can we update treatments and “accepting new clients” ourselves?
Yes. Your team can update specialisation copy, insurance notes and whether you accept new clients — without waiting on a developer when the agenda is full or a new therapist joins.
Do you also support content like exercises or blogs?
Yes. We can structure exercise tips or recovery articles that support trust and search, while keeping the book appointment CTA prominent so content never replaces conversion.
