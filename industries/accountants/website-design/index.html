A generic site loses the entrepreneur who needed a clear fit

Someone googling a bookkeeper for their webshop or a freelancer looking for Moneybird support is not browsing casually. They compare whether a firm names their type of business, which software you work with and how packages are structured — then request an introduction from the office that already feels like a match. Around filing deadlines that comparison speeds up. A site stuck on stock calculators, a vague “full service accounting” line or no software mention sends that high-value relationship to the competitor who spoke their language.

Accountants and bookkeepers do not need a lawyer consultation panic path or a contractor photo gallery. They need service depth (bookkeeping, annual accounts, IB/VPB, payroll, advice), branch niches, software as a trust signal, package shape without public price tags, B2B intake and an explanation of how documents reach the portal. Each new client often stays for years, so a missed fit is expensive. Miss that structure, and you pay in fewer lasting clients — and in entrepreneurs who chose the firm that felt safer for a multi-year relationship.

Referrals still matter, but your website is where you control the narrative: niches, Exact or Twinfield familiarity, how jaarrekening season works and what happens after the first call. An entrepreneur who is unsure about onboarding or portal handover rarely asks for a second meeting. When two practices are equally competent on paper, the one that projects organised reliability online often wins the introduction.

What we build

What is included in an accountant website

Each block serves an entrepreneur choosing who gets their books for years — services, niches, software, packages, B2B intake — not a generic brochure with a calculator icon.

Services split the way entrepreneurs buy

Separate blocks for bookkeeping, annual accounts, income and corporate tax filing, payroll and advisory — each with who it fits and when to start. Freelancers comparing “bookkeeping” versus “jaarrekening support” land on substance, not one vague “accounting services” blob.

Branch niches that self-select clients

Pages or sections for bookkeeper for freelancers, hospitality, e-commerce and SMEs — so a café owner or webshop founder recognises their world before they request an introduction.

Software as a trust signal

Exact, Twinfield, Moneybird, e-Boekhouden and similar named where you actually work — compatibility and familiarity that reassure an entrepreneur already living in that stack.

Package structure without price tags

Clear monthly package tiers — what is included, who each package suits — without publishing figures on the page. Entrepreneurs understand the shape of the relationship before they ask for a quote.

B2B intake for a lasting relationship

A form that captures company type, software, turnover band and services needed — enough to prepare a serious introduction, not a one-click “call me now” panic button.

Client portal and document handover explained

Plain language on how invoices, bank files and payroll docs reach your office — portal, e-mail or app — so switching bookkeeper feels organised rather than chaotic.

References from entrepreneurs like them

Quotes and short stories from freelancers and SME owners who stayed — trust next to niche and software signals when someone is choosing who gets their books for years.

Reachability and reliability on the surface

Response expectations, who to contact for payroll versus tax, and a calm professional tone — the reliability cues relationship buyers scan before they hand over access.

How we work

How an accountant website project runs

From the first walkthrough of niches and packages to measuring which pages drive introduction requests from the clients you want to keep.

01

Discovery in the practice

We map how clients find you today: referrals, Google, software communities and seasonal filing spikes. We note niches you want to grow, packages you offer and where entrepreneurs bounce when they cannot see software or service fit.

02

Service and niche architecture

Information architecture separates bookkeeping, annual accounts, tax filing, payroll, advisory, branch niches and software signals. Package structure and introduction forms each get a defined slot — not one mega-menu labelled “Services”.

03

Design for calm B2B trust

Visual design that signals a steady practice: readable typography, space for software logos and entrepreneur references, CTAs that say “Request an introduction” rather than marketplace urgency.

04

Build, forms, train

We build the site, wire B2B intake and package enquiry paths, place niche and software blocks, and train the team to update services and references without a developer for every new sector page.

05

Launch and measure introductions

Launch tracks introduction requests by niche and service, which package pages entrepreneurs read longest and which software mentions correlate with form starts. You see whether freelancers, hospitality or e-commerce fills the pipeline — not anonymous hits that never become clients.

Scope scales with the number of service and niche pages, package structure, software signals, portal explainers, multilingual needs and how structured your B2B intake must be. A solo bookkeeper with three niches asks for a different build than a firm with payroll plus multiple advisors. Frequently Asked Questions

Expert answers on SEO, AI SEO, Local SEO and digital growth from Amora Digital.

Can I show specialisations per industry niche?
Yes. We structure pages or sections for niches such as freelancers, hospitality, e-commerce and SMEs so entrepreneurs recognise their world before they request an introduction. That self-selection improves lead quality for a long-term relationship.
Can I mention the software I work with?
Yes. Naming Exact, Twinfield, Moneybird, e-Boekhouden or similar where you actually work is a strong trust and compatibility signal. Entrepreneurs already living in that stack often choose the firm that speaks their tools.
How do I attract the right clients with the website?
Lead with who you serve best, which services fit which company type, and how packages are shaped — then use a B2B intake that captures company type, software and needs. Quality rises when poor fits self-select out before onboarding.
Can I show packages without publishing prices?
Yes. We present monthly package tiers with what is included and who each package suits, without figures on the page. Entrepreneurs understand the relationship shape, then request a quote for their situation.
Does this work for a sole practitioner or a larger firm?
Yes. Scope scales from an essential practice site for a focused bookkeeping practice to niche-ready or multi-advisor builds. We map services, niches and intake before fixing deliverables.
Should the site explain a client portal or document handover?
Usually yes. Plain language on how invoices, bank files and payroll docs reach your office reduces switching anxiety. Entrepreneurs want the handover to feel organised, not chaotic.
How long does an accountant website project take?
Most practice websites launch in about four to eight weeks, depending on niche and service depth, package structure and content readiness. Discovery starts with how introductions arrive so we do not ship a generic clinic template.
How is this different from a law firm website?
Accounting sites sell an ongoing B2B relationship around books, tax seasons and software fit. Law firm sites centre practice areas, discreet consultation and case-driven urgency. The structures and proof points are deliberately different.
